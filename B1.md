# B1 — The Glacier cost gotcha

## What the current configuration actually does

terraform/lifecycle.tf configures a transition to storage_class = "GLACIER" at days = 30. In S3 this means **S3 Glacier Flexible Retrieval**, not Glacier Deep Archive or Glacier Instant Retrieval. The transition age is measured from object creation/upload, not the date the listing was sold. Lifecycle processing is asynchronous, so day 30 is eligibility rather than a guaranteed execution time; the assessment scenario assumes the transition actually happens that day.

The seller-deletion deadline appears only in comments: deletion must happen within 30 days of the seller's request. No application deletion worker, request tracking, expiration rule, or deadline enforcement is implemented by this Terraform file. Archival is a storage-class change, not deletion.

The rule is named archive-sold-listing-photos but has no filter or prefix. Its name does not restrict it to sold listings or photos. There is no explicit sold-status selection. The supplied plan warns that the provider requires a filter or prefix and that this omission will become an error in a future provider version. An empty filter would express all-object scope, but would not solve the missing sold-status restriction.

## Day 30 transition and day 40 deletion

| Event | Time | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Photo uploaded | Day 0 | Object age starts |
| Photo transitions to GLACIER | Day 30, as assumed in the scenario | The 90-day archive minimum starts |
| Seller requests deletion; photo is deleted immediately | Day 40 | 10 days spent in Glacier; deletion deadline met |
| Latest permitted deletion for this request | Day 70 | Request day 40 plus 30 days |
| Archive minimum would finish | Day 120 | Transition day 30 plus 90 days |

S3 Glacier Flexible Retrieval has a **90-day minimum storage duration**, measured from entry into that class. Deleting after only 10 days incurs an early-deletion charge for approximately **80 remaining days** of the applicable archive storage minimum. The object is deleted; the remaining minimum is a billing consequence, not a requirement to retain the data.

The cost includes pre-transition storage, applicable transition request charges, storage while archived, and the remaining minimum-duration charge, plus any other applicable storage/request components. Deletion does not require restoring or reading the archived photo, so retrieval charges should not be assumed merely because it is deleted.

There is insufficient information for a dollar estimate: object sizes and count, applicable region and current pricing, and relevant billing details are needed. No dollar amount is asserted here.

Delaying deletion until the latest allowed day 70 would still leave approximately 40 days of the archive minimum unpaid. Waiting until day 120 would avoid that particular early-deletion charge but would breach the day-70 deletion deadline. Storage economics must not delay the required deletion.

## Proposed lifecycle and deletion design

**Recommended default: keep seller-request-deletable photos in S3 Standard and remove their automatic day-30 Glacier transition from the proposed design.** Standard avoids Glacier's 90-day minimum-duration exposure for that content. Compare its higher ongoing storage expense with the full archive costs and actual deletion frequency before selecting a cheaper policy. This is a design recommendation only; no Terraform file has been edited.

A request can arrive at any time, so merely delaying the archive transition, or confirming that no request is currently pending, cannot guarantee 90 more days of retention. Do not assume that a sold listing is safe to archive. Other storage classes must also be evaluated for their own minimum-duration and request/monitoring costs rather than assumed to solve the problem.

The application should record the request time and a deletion deadline of request time plus 30 days, immediately exclude the listing from any archive-eligibility workflow, and queue deletion promptly. A deletion worker should delete the relevant photos, retry failures, verify completion, and alert well before the deadline. Existing archived objects must also be deleted promptly when requested, accepting any remaining minimum-duration charge. Changing the lifecycle rule does not move already-archived objects back to Standard or erase their existing billing exposure.

If bucket versioning or replication is used, the deletion design must cover retained versions and copies according to the deletion requirement: a delete marker alone does not erase previous versions. Those features are not configured in the inspected lifecycle file and must not be assumed present. Avoid adding retention controls that would prevent the required deletion.

## Sold-status filtering and optional archive eligibility

Sold status must be supplied by the application; S3 does not infer it from the rule name. Maintain an object tag such as listing_status = sold when a listing is sold, and explicitly select photos, for example with an application-controlled photos/ prefix where the object-key convention supports it.

If a separately justified archive cohort is retained, its transition rule should use an explicit AND filter combining:

- the photo-object prefix, if supported by the chosen key layout;
- listing_status = sold;
- archive_eligible = true.

The application must maintain these tags and withhold eligibility from deletion-pending content. For the recommended Standard-only cohort, no Glacier transition is enabled at all. The optional archive filter is a restriction on a separately approved archive policy, not an instruction to archive every sold photo.

Only use the archive cohort where the expected retention and full cost model justify the minimum-duration exposure. If those objects remain subject to seller deletion requests, the deadline still takes priority and early-deletion charges remain possible; eligibility tags do not provide a guarantee against future requests. If eliminating that exposure is required, keep all such request-deletable objects out of GLACIER.

Finally, days = 30 still means 30 days from upload even when a sold-status tag filter is added. If the business policy means 30 days after sale, the application must track sold_at and control eligibility at that time; adding a tag does not restart S3's object-age clock. Application tracking is also necessary for the request-relative deletion deadline. An S3 lifecycle transition or expiration alone is not a hard deadline mechanism.

## Decision

Meet the seller's deletion deadline first. Use Standard for the unpredictable, request-deletable cohort; make any archive cohort explicit, sold-filtered, and justified by retention economics. Track early-deletion charges separately when deleting existing archived content so they do not silently inflate the storage budget.
